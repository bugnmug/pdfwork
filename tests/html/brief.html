Launch review · First draft

A first look at the spring launch

Prepared for Team Atlas · 4 March 2026

Based on the project plan, the support backlog and the last three sprint reviews. Marketing figures weren’t final, so they aren’t covered here.

The readThe product is ready, but the support team isn’t staffed for the first month.
What could slipPayment setup in two regions, which depends on a partner we don’t control.
Our betLaunch in the north first, then the south six weeks later.
Your moveAnswer the five questions at the end. Your answers set the date.

01Where things stand

On paper the launch is “on track”: every feature in the plan is built, tested and documented. The real picture is more mixed, because the partner’s payment work is two weeks behind1 and the support rota still has gaps on weekends.

What the team has done well is ship on time for four sprints in a row, with fewer than ten open bugs at any point. That record is the strongest reason to keep the date rather than move it.

The plan doesn’t yet say who answers customers in the first week. Without that, a smooth launch can still feel rough to the people using it.

02What could go wrong

A forecast, not a verdict. Push back where it’s wrong.

Look at the pattern in the sprint reviews: delays came from outside the team, never from the work itself. The risk is concentrated in two places, the payment partner and support cover, and both can be managed.

Reading A · Most likely

The launch goes well in the north and exposes the support gap before the south follows.

Two other readings fit the same facts:

Reading B

The partner slips again

Then the south waits, and nothing else changes.

Reading C

Demand beats every forecast

And the support gap becomes the only story anyone remembers.

The questions at the end are meant to tell these apart.

03How we decide

Step 1

Check the numbers

Pull the last four weeks of bug and ticket counts and compare them with the targets agreed in January.

Step 2

Ask the partner

Get a written date for the payment work and what it depends on.

Step 3

Staff the first month

Fill every weekend shift for four weeks, including cover for illness and leave.

Step 4

Set the date

Pick the launch day only after steps one to three are done and signed off.

05 · The recommendation, before your answers

Option 2, starting in the north.

It uses the team’s strongest record, it keeps the promised date for most customers, and it buys six weeks for the partner. The south follows once payments pass a full test.

06Do this week

Confirm the partner date: a written commitment for the payment work, with the steps it depends on.
Fill the weekend rota for the first four weeks, with a named backup for every shift.
Draft the waiting-list page for customers in the south, in both languages.

07Five questions for the team

A few honest lines each beats a long reply.

1
Which part of the launch worries you most, and what would make it worry you less?
2
If we launch in the north first, who should own each of these?
aThe support rota and its backups
bThe waiting list and messages to customers in the south
cThe partner and the payment tests
dThe second campaign
3
What would change your mind about the date, and how soon would we know?
4
Which promise to customers matters more: the date, or a smooth first week?
5
What should we stop doing to make room for the launch work?
What happens next. Once you answer, we’ll fix the date and share the plan: owners, the support rota, the campaign calendar and the test schedule.
